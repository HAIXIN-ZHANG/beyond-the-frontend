## 3. Distinctions the explanation must preserve

This is an editorial reading lens, not a new technical theory or compulsory chapter form.

- **An enforceable promise has a boundary and conditions.** Locate the rule, trusted mechanism, covered paths and assumptions. Do not imply that correct architecture eliminates all unknowns; equally, do not say no reliable guarantees are possible.
- **An observation or estimate answers a limited question.** A log, observed latency, sample evaluation or passed check is evidence with scope, not a guarantee about every outcome. Observation may also be incomplete.
- **An unknown operation outcome differs from uncertain answer quality.** Not receiving an enrollment response is not evidence of failure. A well-formed model answer can still need semantic judgment. These should not share one undifferentiated “AI is unpredictable” explanation.
- **A work/learning decision has its own uncertainty.** In a hypothetical task, a developer may be unsure what the generated change does or what to practice independently. The useful next step may be a smaller delegated task, primary-source reading, an explanation in their own words, or pausing the change. This is a teaching proposal, not proof that a method improves learning or that delegation always increases understanding burden.
- **Trust is specific to a decision here.** Specify what the person is willing to let a tool do next, on what evidence and within which permissions. A person's relief, confidence or unease can appear as a situated question, not a universal psychological response or a proxy for correctness.

In the existing system cases, preserve state/evidence, limit consequences and establish a proportionate recovery path. In working-with-AI cases, also show the convenience actually sought: reaching an initial version, comparing explanations, inspecting unfamiliar code or drafting checks. The point is useful collaboration with accountable decisions, not a warning catalogue.

## 7. Original recurring cases — before/after explanatory paths

All cases below are hypothetical teaching designs reusing existing course-product contexts. “Before” is a shortcut reading to examine, not a claim that the existing book teaches it. “After” is a planned explanation path, not finished prose or an observed AI result. No one is required to complete a project.

### Case A — AI drafts enrollment; timeout leaves an unknown result

- **Existing base:** G01's lost response and later empty query; homes P4.3/P5.3, UI P2.6/P2.7, checks P6.4, AI delivery P8.10.
- **Convenience sought / shortcut:** use AI to draft the enrollment feature and initial checks; a visible success/error branch and disabled button seem sufficient to the hypothetical developer.
- **Proposed path:** define the saved-enrollment promise → inspect the trusted write/dedup boundaries → response lost after write → retain “not yet confirmed” and operation/state evidence → distinguish duplicate-action refusal from existing enrollment → verify an authorized current/final result before changing the displayed conclusion.
- **Working/learning decision:** ask AI for the relevant call path and a small unknown-response case; the person explains why timeout is not failure and checks the actual change. If they cannot follow where enrollment is written, consult code/source or shrink the task before proceeding. The convenience remains; the delegated scope becomes inspectable.
- **Guarantee/evidence boundary:** invariant enforcement requires explicit covered writes/identity/transaction assumptions in later analysis. G01 already teaches the uncertainty sequence; do not retrofit a second complete account into it or invent measurements.
- **Narrative carrier:** the learner leaves the page without a confirmed result and returns; the developer must decide what the page can truthfully say. Follow that decision into the stored record and the unreturned response, then back to the user's next action. The tension comes from different available facts, not a fabricated disaster or a moral about distrusting AI.

### Case B — AI gives a plausible implementation and valid JSON, but the domain result is wrong

- **Existing base:** P8.2's valid JSON/invalid course ID; P8.7 tool boundaries; P8.8 evals; P8.10 delivery. Opening already separates fluent explanation from truth. Only that opening is written/reviewed; these Part8 units are planned.
- **Convenience sought / shortcut:** obtain an initial course-classification integration plus explanations/checks quickly; the answer looks complete and the format parses, so the hypothetical developer considers handing it to the action path.
- **Proposed path:** split the generated program, generated data and authoritative action → inspect the structure check → examine a nonexistent/ineligible course case → apply the independent business/permission boundary before any consequential operation → retain a refusal/review/fallback state → compare actual task cases instead of treating parse success as correctness.
- **Working/learning decision:** choose whether another model explanation is useful, whether the missing fact requires source/code inspection, or whether to stop action. Keep one small part to implement/explain independently if that is the learning goal. A hypothetical person's unease about a change they cannot explain leads to an inspectable question, not a diagnosis, proof of danger or universal prescription to distrust AI.
- **Guarantee/evidence boundary:** a validation boundary can reject a specified invalid operation under stated implementation conditions; it does not prove all model classifications correct. No provider schema guarantees, improvement percentage or psychological effect is asserted here.
- **Narrative carrier:** a polished generated result initially offers relief; one concrete nonexistent-course example prompts a closer look. That hypothetical shift leads directly to structure versus domain validation and the decision to read, test, revise or pause. The feeling is a local perspective, not evidence about human psychology or model intention.

### Case C — AI sketches upload processing; a repeated task changes the next decision

- **Existing base:** P3.6 callback duplicates, P5.4 worker restart, P5.5 delivery/Outbox, P7.1/P7.3/P7.4 evidence/recovery, P8.5 ingestion and P8.10 implementation.
- **Convenience sought / shortcut:** AI helps draft a web–queue–worker flow and status description; “put it in a queue” is treated as completing reliability and lifecycle design.
- **Proposed path:** name accepted versus completed work → follow stable task identity through repeat/restart → separate processing, index publication and notification effects → inspect which effects may repeat and where dedup/checkpoint responsibilities live → retain recoverable status/evidence → bound retries/impact and reconcile the user-visible result.
- **Working/learning decision:** delegate an initial dependency map or failure-case enumeration, then ask the human to explain one effect boundary and what evidence would confirm completion. If a large generated patch obscures that boundary, compare a smaller slice; do not claim that delegation invariably makes understanding harder or that every system needs more infrastructure.
- **Guarantee/evidence boundary:** covered-effect/storage/ack/checkpoint details require the relevant future analyses. No exactly-once, automatic rollback or guaranteed completion follows from the diagram. Original partial progress can remain useful while a later step is paused.
- **Narrative carrier:** return to the same upload after a worker restart, discover the repeated processing attempt, and follow one effect through status/identity/evidence. Reveal only the needed part of the queue design at each step; keep the human's next action and the user's visible progress in view.

## 8. Voice, cat motif and needed future research

Keep the existing voice of a patient, observant engineering colleague. Show a concrete decision and its consequence, explain the necessary standard term, then change one condition. Benefits and limits should appear in that same example. Respect a reader who is deciding what to learn; no shame about using AI, no reassurance that checking no longer matters, and no compulsory personal-growth lesson.

Give the narrative useful work: let an observation raise a question; slow down for the one boundary/state transition needed to answer it; return to what the person can now decide. Humor can come from the modest mismatch between “done” and what the person still needs to check; avoid rehearsed punchlines or an aphorism after every mechanism. Literary texture can come from concrete detail and pacing, not invented incidents or technical omission.

An experienced voice is a way of observing and explaining, not permission to invent a résumé. Use first-person production experience only when Jason supplies/verifies the event and its publishable scope. Keep the existing course cases identified as fictional once at a useful entry point, then maintain their conditions through actions and diagrams; do not interrupt every paragraph with a new disclaimer. An imagined person's hesitation may motivate a check, but cannot establish a causal psychological claim. Technical terms and standard/implementation limits remain explicit where they affect the explanation.

**Everyday story seed, no incident or wrong answer required:** a developer comfortable with the course UI wants to understand an unfamiliar service module before a small change. AI helps identify an entry point, outline the caller/data path and explain an unfamiliar term. In this hypothetical scene, the developer feels less stuck and becomes curious about one boundary. They compare the explanation with the actual files, choose one relationship to trace personally, and sketch it in their own words before deciding which implementation detail to delegate. The mechanism chain is context selection → proposed explanation → inspected code relationship → retained understanding → next task boundary. The benefit is a usable starting point, not a measured time saving. The scene should still work if the AI explanation is correct: curiosity and a learning choice carry it, rather than a manufactured failure. Review whether the reader can follow that choice, see what AI contributed and explain the relevant system relationship; do not claim confidence or learning improved for readers in general.

The cat can briefly connect understanding/care/trust with a limit on complete control. It does not explain model architecture, prove agency or replace a permission/evidence boundary. If an opening passage is later assigned, a single small image/idea is enough; technical chapters need not mention cats again. Do not invent a pet history, emotional biography or intentional model behavior.

# Licensing and public-project readiness

Updated: 2026-11-16. The project owner selected MIT for project source code and original programming examples, and CC BY-SA 4.0 for original book prose and teaching diagrams. [README](../README.md), [LICENSE](../LICENSE) and [LICENSE-CONTENT.md](../LICENSE-CONTENT.md) state the intended scope. These local changes are not evidence of a public release or deployment.

## Scope and exceptions

- **MIT:** project source code and original programming code examples, including programming snippets in book chapters, unless a specific notice says otherwise. Text diagrams and explanatory prose remain book content even in fenced blocks.
- **CC BY-SA 4.0:** original prose and teaching diagrams in `book/drafts/`, to the extent the rights holder can license them. Attribution and share-alike conditions apply; commercial use is allowed. See [Creative Commons' official terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode).
- **Separately governed:** third-party dependencies, excerpts, images and other materials retain their own terms. Project names, logos and trademarks are not granted by these content notices. The selected licenses cannot grant rights not controlled by their licensors.
- **Other project documents:** internal planning and coordination records are not implicitly identified as book prose by this notice; clarify any desired additional scope separately.

## Contribution and publication checks

[CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) explains how to report issues and propose changes. Contributors should have the rights necessary to offer their work under the relevant license and identify third-party material. This is not a claim that copyright in contributors' work transfers to the project owner, or that existing third-party works were relicensed.

Before integrating or publishing, check the actual work and asset inventory, provenance, applicable notices, code-example boundaries and attribution. Record unresolved rights rather than labeling unverified material as cleared. Preserve contributor credits and individual terms. If future commercial republication requires rights beyond a contribution's license, obtain the necessary permissions.

CC BY-SA is a public license: a person who receives licensed material may retain its permissions under its terms even if the owner later changes the license for a new version. See the [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/). A free reading site and any future paid service are separate product choices.

A new umbrella project/brand name, dedicated logo design and further commercial expansion are future Goal 4 considerations, to revisit in light of actual content, readership and needs; they are not current design or implementation assignments.
